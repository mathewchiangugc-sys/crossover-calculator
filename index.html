The Idea
It's the point where your investments can pay you more each month than you spend. Your number is $1,050,000: one year of spending divided by your withdrawal rate. The idea comes from the book Your Money or Your Life.
Why It Matters
Retirement feels far off and fuzzy. The crossover gives you an actual date: age 51. Each dollar you don't spend helps twice. It grows your portfolio, and it lowers the number you need. Once you're there, your job becomes a choice.

The Fine Print
FOR REFERENCE AND EDUCATION ONLY. A MAP OF THE MATH, NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE. Crossover = first year portfolio × withdrawal rate ≥ a year of spending. Everything is in today's dollars: the return is after inflation, and your pay and spending keep pace with it. Coast = enough invested to reach your crossover number by 65 with no more contributions. Monthly compounding, contributions at month end. No taxes, fees or raises beyond the what-ifs. The 4% rule was built for ~30-year retirements; retiring early, many use 3–3.5%. Hitting the crossover isn't a reason to quit on the spot. Markets dip and costs change, so most people keep a buffer. Arithmetic, not a forecast.
